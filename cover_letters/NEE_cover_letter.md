## Core argument

Climate extremes are reshaping how ecosystems function, not only how much they grow. Yet large-scale ecological monitoring still relies on greenness indices and broadband reflectance that cannot resolve which trait dimensions of canopy function have shifted. Apparent greenness recovery after drought, heat or compound climate extremes can hide altered leaf economics, hydraulic regulation or nutrient status—and with them, the ecological meaning of "recovery" itself. We argue that ecosystem resilience and recovery should be measured in **trait space**, not greenness space, and propose **continuous functional observables**: landscape-scale phenotypic state variables that integrate organismal traits, community composition and environmental forcing.

## Theoretical contribution to ecology

Our Perspective is built around three coupled theoretical claims that we believe extend recent NEE Perspectives on remote sensing of ecology and biodiversity (Cavender-Bares et al. 2022 *Nat Ecol Evol*; Skidmore et al. 2021 *Nat Ecol Evol*) into a dynamic-ecology framework:

1. **A dynamic extension of trait ecology.** The leaf economics spectrum (Wright et al. 2004) and the global plant form–function spectrum (Díaz et al. 2016) describe static, cross-species, species-mean axes. Continuous functional observables convert these axes into dynamic, intraspecific, sub-seasonal trajectories at landscape scale—the temporal extension that trait ecology has lacked.

2. **Intraspecific trait variability (ITV) becomes a landscape-scale state variable.** Violle et al. (2012), Siefert et al. (2015) and Westerband et al. (2021) established ITV as community ecology's hidden dimension, but its quantification has been plot-bound. Continuous functional observables make ITV observable across landscapes and seasons, opening empirical tests of response-diversity, redundancy and stability hypotheses that have been largely theoretical.

3. **Resilience operationalized in trait space.** Resistance, recovery and legacy effects (Holling 1973; Pimm 1984; Hodgson et al. 2015; Donohue et al. 2016) have long been theoretical constructs whose empirical implementations from remote sensing have substituted greenness anomalies for the underlying functional state. Continuous functional observables provide the missing measurement layer: functional recovery becomes the return of trait distributions to the pre-disturbance state within uncertainty, not the return of canopy area.

The Perspective also introduces **falsifiable predictions** for the trait fingerprints of common climate stressors (soil drought, heatwave, compound drought–heat, nutrient limitation, biotic outbreak), each specified with timing and magnitude, so that the framework can be tested at the same level of resolution at which it is proposed.

## Empirical anchor: the 2022 European drought

The manuscript is anchored to the 2022 European drought, the first continental-scale climate extreme observed by spaceborne imaging spectroscopy in flight (EnMAP began routine operations in April 2022, just before the drought peak). The illustration documents a setting where the one-year-scale greenness recovery that dominates remote-sensing-based resilience metrics masks demonstrable functional legacy at the trait-space scale ecological theory requires.

## Why now

PRISMA, EnMAP, EMIT and DESIS define the contemporary trait-snapshot era; CHIME (ESA, projected ~2029) and SBG (NASA, projected ~2030+) will multiply hyperspectral observational capacity but cannot retroactively densify the existing archive. The 2026–2030 window is an ecological opportunity, not only a technological one: it is the period in which the ecological community can define functional-diversity, ITV and trait-space recovery observables before mission-specific provenance hardens and fragments the evidence base.

## European forest illustration

A compact European forest example (Section 5) demonstrates trait-specific feasibility (strong for LAI, intermediate for LMA, nitrogen and lignin, weaker for chlorophyll) and reframes this trait-by-trait pattern as the framework's intended ecological readout. A full methods + benchmark paper is in preparation for *Remote Sensing of Environment*; the underlying data product will be archived through a companion *Earth System Science Data* submission.

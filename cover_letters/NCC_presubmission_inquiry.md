Dear Editors,

We write to inquire whether *Nature Climate Change* would be interested in considering a Perspective manuscript titled

> **From greenness to function in ecosystem monitoring: continuous trait observables for detecting and attributing climate-driven functional change.**

## Argument

Climate change is no longer just changing how much vegetation grows; it is changing how vegetation functions. Yet most satellite-based monitoring still relies on greenness indices, broadband reflectance, sun-induced chlorophyll fluorescence (SIF) and near-infrared reflectance of vegetation (NIRv). None of these resolves which trait dimensions of canopy function—photosynthetic capacity, water status, leaf economics or nutrient regulation—have changed during or after a climate extreme. This functional opacity is becoming a measurement bottleneck for climate-impact attribution.

We argue that ecosystem monitoring should be built around **continuous functional observables**: spatially and temporally explicit estimates of plant functional traits, anchored by imaging spectroscopy (PRISMA, EnMAP, EMIT, and the upcoming CHIME and SBG missions) and carried by dense Sentinel-2 and Landsat-era archives. The Perspective proposes a framework distinguishing observed, spectrally anchored, ecologically inferred, climate-context inferred and structurally complemented information; six principles for credible functional observables; and a stress-syndrome diagnostic frame linking common climate stressors (soil drought, heatwave, compound drought–heat, nutrient limitation, biotic outbreak) to testable trait fingerprints.

## Why now

The 2026–2030 window is decisive. PRISMA, EnMAP, EMIT and DESIS define the contemporary trait-snapshot era; CHIME (ESA, projected ~2029) and SBG (NASA, projected ~2030+) will multiply hyperspectral observational capacity but cannot retroactively densify the historical archive. Establishing community standards on continuous functional observables before these missions arrive is an immediate priority for climate-impact attribution and policy-relevant ecosystem monitoring (IPCC AR7 Working Group II, EU Green Deal Forest Strategy 2030, CBD Global Biodiversity Framework, FAO Forest Resources Assessment).

## Proof-of-concept role

A European forest example illustrates trait-specific feasibility (strong for LAI, intermediate for LMA/nitrogen/lignin, weaker for chlorophyll). The trait-by-trait variation is framed as the framework's intended differentiated readout, not as an operational benchmark. Companion methods (Remote Sensing of Environment) and data (Earth System Science Data) papers are in preparation.

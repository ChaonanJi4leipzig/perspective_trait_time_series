## Core argument

Climate change is no longer just changing how much vegetation grows; it is changing how vegetation functions. Yet most satellite-based ecosystem monitoring still relies on greenness indices and broadband reflectance archives that detect canopy browning but cannot diagnose which functional dimensions—photosynthetic capacity, water status, leaf economics, nutrient regulation—have shifted. Sun-induced chlorophyll fluorescence (SIF) and near-infrared reflectance of vegetation (NIRv) have added partial functional capability by linking observations more directly to photosynthetic activity, but they remain integrative proxies that cannot identify which trait dimensions changed. This functional opacity is becoming a measurement bottleneck for climate-impact attribution.

## Contribution

Our Perspective proposes that ecosystem monitoring should be built around **continuous functional observables**: spatially and temporally explicit estimates of plant functional traits, anchored in imaging spectroscopy (PRISMA, EnMAP, EMIT and the upcoming CHIME and SBG missions), carried by dense Sentinel-2 and Landsat-era archives, and complemented by active structural observations from LiDAR, SAR and InSAR. We distinguish directly observed, spectrally anchored, ecologically inferred, climate-context inferred and structurally complemented information, and we propose six principles for credible functional observables. We introduce a **stress-syndrome diagnostic frame** in which common climate stressors—soil drought, heatwaves, compound drought–heat, nutrient limitation, biotic outbreaks—are mapped to testable trait fingerprints, replacing the single-index anomalies that climate-impact attribution has often relied on.

## Why now

The 2026–2030 window is decisive. PRISMA, EnMAP, EMIT and DESIS define the contemporary trait-snapshot era (2019–2028). CHIME (ESA, projected ~2029) and SBG (NASA, projected ~2030+) will multiply hyperspectral observational capacity but cannot retroactively densify the existing archive. Establishing community standards on continuous functional observables before CHIME/SBG data dominate the conversation is a community priority that climate science, IPCC working groups and biodiversity-monitoring frameworks have started to articulate but have not yet had a framework around which to coalesce.

## Proof-of-concept role

A European forest illustration (Section 4 and Figure 3) demonstrates trait-specific feasibility (strong for LAI, intermediate for LMA/nitrogen/lignin, weaker for chlorophyll). This trait-specific patterning is presented as the framework's intended differentiated readout, not as an operational benchmark. A full methods + benchmark paper is in preparation for *Remote Sensing of Environment*, and the underlying data product will be archived through a companion *Earth System Science Data* submission.

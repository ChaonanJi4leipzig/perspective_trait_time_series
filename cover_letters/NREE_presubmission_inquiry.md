# Presubmission inquiry draft for Nature Reviews Earth & Environment

Dear Editors,

We would like to inquire whether the proposed Perspective, **"Trait data cubes for ecosystem monitoring"**, may be suitable for consideration in *Nature Reviews Earth & Environment*.

Ecosystem monitoring has become increasingly continuous in space and time, yet much operational interpretation still relies on greenness and structural proxies. These variables are invaluable, but they often conflate canopy structure, pigment dynamics, water limitation, phenology and viewing geometry. At the same time, imaging spectroscopy is making plant functional traits observable from space, while multispectral missions provide dense temporal archives. Our Perspective argues that the next step is to combine these complementary observing systems into **trait data cubes**: continuous, uncertainty-aware functional observables anchored by sparse imaging spectroscopy and carried through time by dense multispectral records.

The manuscript is not intended as a broad literature review of hyperspectral remote sensing. Instead, it develops a forward-looking framework for trait-based ecosystem monitoring. We focus on four conceptual contributions:

1. why ecosystem monitoring needs functional observables beyond greenness;
2. how imaging spectroscopy can provide trait anchors while multispectral archives provide temporal support;
3. how trait cubes could enable stress-syndrome attribution, resistance, recovery, legacy-effect analysis and dynamic functional diversity monitoring;
4. which guardrails are needed for operational products, including uncertainty reporting, domain-of-applicability masks and validation across sites, years and ecological contexts.

An early European forest proof-of-concept is included only as an illustration that the sparse-to-dense framework is technically implementable with current Earth observation archives. It is not presented as a complete benchmark or as the main contribution of the article. The broader argument is global and conceptual: trait data cubes could provide a route from spectral snapshots to functional ecosystem monitoring as PRISMA, EnMAP, EMIT, CHIME and SBG expand the imaging-spectroscopy record.

We believe the manuscript is timely for *Nature Reviews Earth & Environment* because the field is approaching a transition from mission-driven spectral data products to ecology-facing functional monitoring products. The article would speak to remote sensing scientists, ecologists, Earth system modellers and environmental monitoring communities.

The planned article length is approximately 3,500--4,500 words, with four to five figures:

1. the conceptual shift from greenness proxies to functional observables;
2. the sparse-to-dense trait-cube framework;
3. a compact European forest proof-of-concept;
4. trait anomalies for stress, resistance, recovery and legacy effects;
5. a roadmap for operational trait-cube monitoring.

We would be grateful for your advice on whether this Perspective would be of interest to the journal.

Sincerely,

Chaonan Ji, on behalf of the co-authors

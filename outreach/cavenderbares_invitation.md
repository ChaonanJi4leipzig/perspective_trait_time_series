## Step 2 — formal invitation from Chaonan to Jeannine (after Miguel's intro lands)

Subject: Trait-space recovery as an ecological state variable — invitation to engage with a *Nat Ecol Evol* Perspective

Dear Professor Cavender-Bares,

Thank you for the introduction Miguel sent. I am writing to invite you to engage with a Perspective I am preparing for *Nature Ecology & Evolution*: **"From greenness to functional trait space: continuous trait observables for ecosystem recovery in a changing climate"** (working title).

The Perspective makes three connected claims:

1. **Ecosystem recovery after climate extremes must be measured in trait space, not greenness space.** Apparent greenness recovery after drought, heatwave or compound climate extremes can hide altered leaf economics, hydraulic regulation or nutrient status; the ecological meaning of "recovery" depends on which state space we use to define it.

2. **Continuous functional observables operationalize the leaf economics spectrum and the global plant form–function spectrum as dynamic, intraspecific frameworks**, converting their static, species-mean axes into landscape-scale trajectories.

3. **Intraspecific trait variability and dynamic functional diversity become observable as ecosystem-scale state variables for the first time** — a contribution that, to my knowledge, has not been made in any previous remote-sensing-of-ecology Perspective.

The 2022 European drought serves as a concrete test case: it is the first continental-scale climate extreme observed by spaceborne imaging spectroscopy in flight (EnMAP began routine operations in April 2022, just before the drought peak), and it is anchored to the falsifiable predictions developed in the Perspective.

Your 2022 *NEE* Perspective with Schneider, Santos, Armstrong and colleagues is the most direct lineage for this work. You explicitly identified the phenology–dynamics gap (`"remote sensing will offer the capacity for unravelling these relationships … in a manner that is not practical with in situ data collection"`), and the present Perspective is, in essence, the operationalization of that capacity. I would like the manuscript to engage with your framework as a substantive antecedent rather than a citation in passing.

**Three concrete asks, in order of imposition:**

1. **Pre-submission read and feedback** (~2 weeks before submission). I would deeply value your reaction to whether the trait-space-recovery framing extends or misreads the integrative framework you proposed in 2022. Any feedback you can offer at that stage would shape the final manuscript.

2. **Co-authorship**, if the Perspective develops in a direction you would want to be associated with. The manuscript is currently 4,500 words with 5 figures and 6 boxes; I would welcome your editorial and conceptual input, and I would expect substantial revision in response to your read. Your contribution could focus on connecting the trait-space framework to cross-biome and cross-continental contexts beyond European forests, where your work on tropical-versus-temperate trait variation would substantially strengthen the empirical scope.

3. If neither of the above is possible at this time, your **acknowledgement of having seen a draft** would be welcome, and we would naturally cite your 2022 work as the most immediate lineage for ours.

I have attached the current manuscript draft, the 1-page presubmission pitch (`NEE_pitch.md`), and the cover letter draft for context. I would be very grateful for any response, including a quick "not at this time" if your bandwidth does not allow.

With great respect,

Chaonan Ji
MSCA Postdoctoral Fellow
ENS Paris-Saclay & LSCE/IPSL

cc: Miguel D. Mahecha (Leipzig), Philippe Ciais (LSCE)

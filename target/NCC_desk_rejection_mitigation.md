# Desk rejection risks at Nature Climate Change and mitigation

## Risk 1: Manuscript reads as a remote-sensing methods paper, not a climate-impact paper

Mitigation (already implemented in Day 1–7 revisions):

- Title, abstract first sentence, and Section 1 paragraph 1 all lead with climate-impact attribution framing ("Climate change is no longer just changing where and when vegetation grows; it is changing how vegetation functions.").
- Section 5 retitled "What continuous functional observables enable for climate science."
- Section 5.1 promoted with new Box 3 mapping climate stressors → trait fingerprints.
- Imaging spectroscopy and Sentinel-2 framed as enabling technology, not subject.

## Risk 2: Editor sees the proof-of-concept as original research

Mitigation:

- Section 4 retitled "A European forest illustration of trait-specific feasibility" — the word "proof-of-concept" removed.
- Box 2 reframed: "Where trait illustration meets the framework"; chlorophyll-weak result presented as framework readout, not benchmark failure.
- Figure 3 caption explicitly states: "These panels are illustrative, not an operational benchmark."
- Cover letter notes that the full benchmark + methods paper is a separate forthcoming submission to *Remote Sensing of Environment*, and the data product to *Earth System Science Data*.

## Risk 3: SIF/NIRv community sees the framing as a strawman

Mitigation:

- Section 1 paragraph 2 explicitly acknowledges SIF (Sun et al. 2017, *Science*) and NIRv (Badgley et al. 2017, *Science Advances*) as partial functional capabilities.
- Section 1 paragraph 5 ("no current observing system solves this problem alone") explicitly mentions SIF and NIRv as photosynthesis-activity proxies that do not resolve trait dimensions.
- Frame the gap as **trait-dimensional resolution**, not photosynthetic activity per se.

## Risk 4: Trait ecologists see the framework as ignoring decades of trait theory

Mitigation:

- Section 1 paragraph 4 cites Wright et al. 2004 *Nature* (leaf economics spectrum) and Díaz et al. 2016 *Nature* (global functional gradients).
- Position the framework as translating trait ecology into a continuous satellite measurement system, not replacing it.

## Risk 5: Editor doubts cross-mission harmonization claim

Mitigation:

- Section 2 last paragraph and Section 7 first paragraph give an explicit 2026–2030 community window with named missions and projected launch dates (CHIME ~2029, SBG ~2030+).
- Cross-mission harmonization is one of the six principles (Box 4) rather than a claimed achievement.

## Risk 6: Reviewer disputes trait-specific failure modes (chlorophyll weakness)

Mitigation:

- Reframe trait-by-trait variation as framework feature, not bug (Box 2; Figure 3 caption).
- Cite Ji et al. 2024 *New Phytologist* transferability work and Ji et al. 2025 *RSE* PRISMA seasonal study to show this is a community-wide pattern.
- Principle "validate ecological use, not only accuracy" (Box 4 principle 5) addresses this directly.

## Risk 7: Policy hook seen as overreach

Mitigation:

- Section 7 mentions EU Green Deal Forest Strategy 2030, IPCC AR7 Working Group II, CBD GBF and FAO FRA, but frames the policy connection as supportive evidence for the framework's downstream relevance, not as established usage.
- Acknowledge in cover letter that policy uptake will require future demonstration via the companion methods/data papers.

## Risk 8: Reviewer challenges climate-attribution depth (NCC-specific risk)

Mitigation:

- Section 5.2 explicitly links trait-based legacy detection to climate-attribution chains (forcing → ecosystem response → impact).
- Section 6 explicitly states that uncertainty under-reporting is more dangerous than over-reporting in attribution use.
- Box 4 principle 6 ("Document climate context for attribution use") makes attribution-readiness a product requirement.

## Risk 9: 11-author manuscript appears assembled rather than coherent

Mitigation:

- All co-authors are part of the same active research programme (Chaonan + Ciais lab + Mahecha/Feilhauer Leipzig group + Cherif upstream model).
- Cover letter clarifies that Chaonan leads, with named contributions from each co-author.
- Limit corresponding author to Chaonan Ji and Philippe Ciais.

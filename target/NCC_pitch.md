# Presubmission pitch: Nature Climate Change

## Proposed title

**From greenness to function in ecosystem monitoring: continuous trait observables for detecting and attributing climate-driven functional change**

## Article type

Perspective, approximately 4,000–4,500 words with five display items and four boxes (definition + observed/anchored/inferred/complemented matrix + climate stressor → trait fingerprint table + six principles).

## Core argument

Climate change is not just changing how much vegetation grows—it is changing how vegetation functions. Heatwaves, multi-year droughts and compound climate extremes drive distinct functional responses (pigment composition, water regulation, leaf economics, nutrient status) that current greenness-based monitoring cannot diagnose. Sun-induced chlorophyll fluorescence (SIF) and near-infrared reflectance of vegetation (NIRv) have added partial functional capability by linking observations more directly to photosynthetic activity, but they remain integrative proxies that cannot identify which trait dimensions of canopy function have changed.

This functional opacity is becoming a measurement bottleneck for climate-impact attribution. We propose a framework for moving from sparse hyperspectral trait snapshots to continuous functional trait observables, anchored by imaging spectroscopy (PRISMA/EnMAP/EMIT and the upcoming CHIME/SBG missions), carried by dense Sentinel-2 and Landsat-era archives, and complemented by active structural observations from LiDAR, SAR and InSAR.

## Why now (climate framing)

The CHIME-SBG era opens in 2028–2030, multiplying hyperspectral observational capacity but unable to retroactively densify the existing archive. The 2026–2030 window is decisive for establishing community standards on continuous functional observables before new missions impose their own provenance assumptions. At the same time, climate-impact attribution—central to IPCC AR7 Working Group II, the EU Green Deal Forest Strategy 2030, the CBD Global Biodiversity Framework, and the FAO Forest Resources Assessment—increasingly requires trait-level evidence currently missing from greenness-based reporting.

## What is new

1. A framework that distinguishes **directly observed**, **spectrally anchored**, **ecologically inferred**, **climate-context inferred** and **structurally complemented** information.
2. **Six principles** for credible functional observables, including explicit climate-context documentation enabling attribution use.
3. A **stress-syndrome diagnostic frame**: trait fingerprints of common climate stressors (soil drought, heatwave, compound drought–heat, nutrient limitation, biotic outbreak) as testable hypotheses, replacing single-index anomalies.

## Proof-of-concept role

A European forest illustration (Section 4 and Figure 3) demonstrates trait-specific feasibility (strong for LAI, intermediate for LMA/nitrogen/lignin, weaker for chlorophyll). This trait-specific patterning is framed as the framework's intended differentiated readout, not as an operational benchmark. A full methods + benchmark paper is in preparation for *Remote Sensing of Environment*, and the underlying data product will be archived through a companion *Earth System Science Data* submission.

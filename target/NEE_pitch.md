# Presubmission pitch: Nature Ecology & Evolution

## Proposed title

**From greenness to functional trait space: continuous trait observables for ecosystem recovery in a changing climate**

## Article type

Perspective, approximately 4,000–4,500 words with five display items and five boxes (information classes for ecological interpretation; European forest illustration; falsifiable trait fingerprints of climate stressors; six principles for credible functional ecological observables; functional diversity observables from trait maps).

## Core argument

Climate extremes are reshaping how ecosystems function, not only how much they grow. Apparent greenness recovery after drought, heatwave or compound extremes can hide altered leaf economics, hydraulic regulation or nutrient status — and with them, the ecological meaning of "recovery." We argue that ecosystem resilience and recovery should be measured in **trait space**, not greenness space, and propose **continuous functional observables**: landscape-scale phenotypic state variables that integrate organismal traits, community composition and environmental forcing, and whose temporal trajectories carry ecological information not reducible to any single underlying trait.

## Theoretical contribution (the NEE-specific advance)

The Perspective is built around three coupled theoretical claims that we believe NEE readers will recognize as overdue advances in trait-based ecology:

1. **A dynamic extension of trait ecology.** The leaf economics spectrum (Wright et al. 2004) and the global plant form–function spectrum (Díaz et al. 2016) describe static, cross-species, species-mean axes of plant strategy. Continuous functional observables convert these axes into dynamic, intraspecific, sub-seasonal trajectories at landscape scale — the temporal extension that trait ecology has lacked for fifty years.

2. **Intraspecific trait variability (ITV) becomes a landscape-scale state variable.** Violle et al. (2012), Siefert et al. (2015) and others established that ITV is community ecology's hidden dimension, but its quantification has been plot-bound. Trait observables make ITV observable across landscapes and seasons, opening empirical tests of response-diversity, redundancy and stability hypotheses that have been largely theoretical.

3. **Resilience operationalized in trait space.** Resistance, recovery and legacy effects (Pimm 1984; Holling 1973; Hodgson et al. 2015; Donohue et al. 2016) have long been theoretical constructs whose empirical implementations from remote sensing have substituted greenness anomalies for the underlying functional state. Continuous functional observables provide the missing measurement layer: functional recovery becomes return to the pre-disturbance trait distribution within uncertainty, not return of canopy area.

## Why now

Imaging spectroscopy is entering an operational phase through PRISMA, EnMAP and EMIT, with CHIME and SBG launching 2028–2030. The 2022 European drought is the first major continental-scale climate extreme observed by spaceborne imaging spectroscopy in flight (EnMAP launched April 2022), making it a natural anchoring case for a Perspective on trait-space recovery. The community window for establishing ecological-interpretation standards for functional trait observables is now, before mission-specific provenance fragments the evidence base.

## What is new

1. A sharper ecological definition of "continuous functional observable" as a landscape-scale phenotypic state variable — emergent from organismal traits, community composition and environmental forcing — distinct from both organism-scale traits and pixel-scale spectral indices.
2. **Falsifiable predictions** for the trait fingerprints of common climate stressors (soil drought, heatwave, compound drought–heat, nutrient limitation, biotic outbreak), each specified with timing and magnitude.
3. A **trait readiness × ecological process matrix** that maps observation feasibility to ecological interpretation reliability, rather than to model accuracy alone.
4. A **functional diversity observables framework** (community-weighted means, dispersion, ITV, turnover, response diversity, redundancy) that connects trait monitoring to community-ecology and biodiversity science.

## European forest illustration role

A compact European forest illustration shows trait-specific feasibility (strong for LAI, intermediate for LMA/N/lignin, weaker for chlorophyll), anchored ecologically by a 2022 European drought vignette. The illustration is framed as the framework's intended differentiated readout, not as an operational benchmark. A full methods + benchmark paper is in preparation for *Remote Sensing of Environment*; the underlying data product is being archived through a companion *Earth System Science Data* submission.

## Lineage and audience

The Perspective extends the lineage of recent NEE and Nature Plants Perspectives on remote sensing of plant function (Cavender-Bares et al. 2022 *Nat Ecol Evol*; Skidmore et al. 2021 *Nat Ecol Evol*; Schweiger et al. 2018; Jetz et al. 2016 *Nat Plants*; Yan et al. 2025 *Nat Plants*), and is written for ecologists working on functional diversity, community ecology, ecosystem resilience and global change biology, with relevance to Earth observation and Earth-system modelling communities.

# Presubmission pitch: Nature Reviews Earth & Environment

## Proposed title

Trait data cubes for ecosystem monitoring

## Article type

Perspective / Comment proposal.

## Core argument

Ecosystem monitoring remains dominated by greenness and structural proxies, while climate extremes require functional observables that can distinguish stress pathways, recovery and legacy effects. Imaging spectroscopy can anchor plant functional traits, but hyperspectral observations remain sparse in time. We propose trait data cubes as a framework that uses sparse imaging-spectroscopy trait anchors and dense multispectral archives to build continuous, uncertainty-aware functional observables.

## Why now

Spaceborne imaging spectroscopy is entering an operational phase through PRISMA, EnMAP, EMIT and upcoming CHIME/SBG missions. At the same time, ecological monitoring increasingly needs functional indicators for drought response, resilience and Earth system model evaluation. The field needs a synthesis that defines how sparse spectral trait retrievals can become continuous monitoring products.

## What is new

The manuscript does not review all hyperspectral methods. It reframes imaging spectroscopy as a trait-anchor system and argues that the next step is continuous trait-cube monitoring with uncertainty, provenance and ecological validation.

## Proof-of-concept role

A European forest testbed is included as a compact proof-of-concept showing early reconstruction of selected hyperspectral-derived traits from Sentinel-2 temporal context. It is not presented as a final benchmark, and the internal project name should not be foregrounded in the manuscript.

# Presubmission pitch: Nature Reviews Earth & Environment

## Proposed title

From greenness to function in ecosystem monitoring

## Article type

Perspective proposal, approximately 4,000--4,500 words with five display items.

## Core argument

Ecosystem monitoring remains dominated by greenness proxies, while climate extremes require functional observables that can distinguish stress pathways, recovery and legacy effects. Imaging spectroscopy can anchor biochemical and optical plant traits, but hyperspectral observations remain sparse in time and should not be framed as a direct source of detailed three-dimensional canopy structure. We propose a framework for moving from spectral snapshots to functional ecosystem monitoring by combining imaging-spectroscopy trait anchors, dense multispectral temporal archives and complementary structural observations from LiDAR, SAR and InSAR.

## Why now

Spaceborne imaging spectroscopy is entering an operational phase through PRISMA, EnMAP, EMIT and upcoming CHIME/SBG-class missions. At the same time, ecological monitoring increasingly needs functional indicators for drought response, resilience and Earth-system model evaluation. The field needs a Perspective that defines how sparse trait retrievals can become continuous monitoring products without overstating what spectroscopy directly observes.

## What is new

The manuscript does not review all hyperspectral methods. It reframes imaging spectroscopy as a trait-anchor system and argues that the next step is continuous functional monitoring with uncertainty, provenance, domain-of-applicability masks and ecological validation. A key boundary is the distinction between spectrally anchored traits and traits inferred through trait covariation, temporal context, canopy state and ecological correlation.

## Proof-of-concept role

A European forest testbed is included as a compact proof-of-concept showing early reconstruction of selected hyperspectral-derived traits from Sentinel-2 temporal context. It is not presented as a final benchmark, and the internal project name should not be foregrounded in the manuscript.

# Desk rejection risks and mitigation plan

## Risk 1: The editor sees the paper as original research or a case study

Mitigation:
- Submit a proposal synopsis first, not the full manuscript as an unsolicited research article.
- Keep the proof-of-concept compact and explicitly illustrative.
- State in the abstract, inquiry and main text that the contribution is a Perspective framework.
- Move detailed methods, full 20-trait results and benchmarking details to supplementary notes or future research papers.

## Risk 2: The proof-of-concept looks too local to support a global claim

Mitigation:
- Frame the European forest testbed as proof-of-concept only.
- Make the global claim conceptual: the need for continuous functional observables is global, but the current analysis illustrates feasibility.
- Add a short supplementary methods note with trait list, sensors, test region, split design, metrics and limitations.
- Avoid language such as "operational", "global product" or "validated monitoring system" unless directly supported.

## Risk 3: The novelty looks like another hyperspectral trait retrieval review

Mitigation:
- Lead with the monitoring problem, not with hyperspectral methods.
- Emphasize the shift from trait snapshots to functional time series.
- Make clear that trait retrieval remains essential and active; the Perspective adds the monitoring layer that connects retrievals to dense archives.
- Use key references selectively to establish maturity and limits, rather than reviewing all retrieval methods.
- Keep the central novelty as five principles for functional time series: anchoring, temporal carriers, observed/anchored/inferred/complemented variables, uncertainty/domain limits and ecological validation.

## Risk 4: The paper overclaims what hyperspectral imagery can measure

Mitigation:
- Keep the observed/anchored/inferred/complemented distinction visible in the main text.
- State that hyperspectral imagery is not a direct three-dimensional structure sensor.
- Use LiDAR/SAR/InSAR as the correct structural complement.
- Report trait-specific uncertainty and domain-of-applicability rather than presenting all traits as equally observed.

## Risk 5: The display-item package does not look mature enough for a Nature Reviews journal

Mitigation:
- Use the generated Figure 1 as the current flagship concept and refine it visually before submission.
- Keep Figures 2-5 in consistent Nature-style formatting.
- Reduce figure text and make each figure answer one editorial question.
- Use figure captions to reinforce the Perspective argument rather than describe technical details.
- Treat any schematic time-series panel as product design unless real site-level data are supplied.

## Risk 6: The proposal does not match NREE submission expectations

Mitigation:
- Provide a 200-word abstract, main-section outline, key references and author affiliations.
- Keep the inquiry concise and editor-facing.
- Use the optional figure-preview PDF only as supporting material if the submission system allows it.
- Do not submit simultaneously to another journal.

## What the author team should do next

1. Confirm author order, affiliations and whether all listed co-authors agree to be included in the proposal.
2. Confirm which 20 traits exist, which five to eight traits are strongest, and which should remain supplementary.
3. Decide whether the generated Figure 1 is strong enough for inquiry or whether Chaonan's original hyperspectral-trait concept should be redrawn into the same visual system.
4. Ask two or three senior co-authors for a 15-minute read of the proposal synopsis before submission.
5. Decide whether to include display-item previews in the NREE proposal attachment.

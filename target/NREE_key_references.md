# NREE key references

This list is for the proposal synopsis and editor-facing inquiry. It should remain selective. The manuscript should not read as a broad literature review.

1. Pettorelli et al. on satellite remote sensing for ecology and biodiversity monitoring.
   - Use: establishes why satellite monitoring matters, while leaving room for the argument that ecological interpretation still needs better functional variables.

2. Zeng et al. on optical vegetation indices.
   - Use: supports the claim that greenness indices are powerful but mechanistically ambiguous.

3. Cavender-Bares et al. on spectral biology.
   - Use: positions the manuscript in a broader field-level transition from spectra to biological organization.

4. Cherif et al. on transferable multi-trait models.
   - Use: shows that spectra-to-trait modelling is now mature enough to support a Perspective about monitoring.

5. Mederer et al. on plant trait retrieval and curated hyperspectral data.
   - Use: supports the importance of data curation, trait calibration and community datasets.

6. Fujiang Ji et al. on PLSR transferability.
   - Use: objective evidence that calibration diversity and domain awareness are central constraints.

7. Fujiang Ji et al. on transfer learning for leaf traits.
   - Use: demonstrates a concrete route for improving spatial, species and temporal generalization.

8. Fujiang Ji et al. on PRISMA and NEON seasonal trait variability.
   - Use: evidence that spaceborne imaging spectroscopy is moving from aspiration to practical trait monitoring.

9. Dubayah et al. and Lang et al. on structural remote sensing.
   - Use: protects the manuscript from overstating hyperspectral structure retrieval; structure should be constrained by LiDAR/SAR/InSAR where needed.

10. Liu et al. on spectral phenology or Sentinel-2 trait temporal context.
   - Use: supports the sparse-to-dense temporal reconstruction argument.

### Three NEE-specific theoretical contributions (must be made explicit)
1. **Dynamic extension of trait ecology canon** — LES (Wright 2004) and global form–function spectrum (Díaz 2016) describe *static, cross-species, species-mean* axes. Continuous functional observables convert these into *dynamic, intraspecific, sub-seasonal* trajectories at landscape scale. This is a 50-year-overdue temporal extension.
2. **Landscape-scale ITV (intraspecific trait variability) becomes observable** — Violle 2012 / Siefert 2015 / Westerband 2021 argued ITV is community ecology's hidden dimension, but its quantification has been plot-bound. Trait observables make ITV an ecosystem-scale state variable.
3. **Resilience theory operationalized in trait space** — Pimm 1984 / Holling 1973 / Hodgson 2015 / Donohue 2016 defined resistance/recovery/legacy as theoretical constructs. Trait time series give them empirical measurement endpoints distinct from greenness recovery.

### Abstract rewrite (target: ~200 words, ecology-first)

**Draft v0 (to refine):**
> Climate change is reshaping how ecosystems function, not only how much they grow. Yet large-scale ecological monitoring still relies on greenness indices and broadband reflectance that cannot resolve which trait dimensions of canopy function have shifted. Apparent greenness recovery after drought, heat or compound extremes can hide altered leaf economics, hydraulic regulation or nutrient status — and with them, the meaning of ecosystem "resilience." Here we argue that ecosystem resilience and recovery should be measured in **trait space**, not greenness space, and propose **continuous functional observables** — landscape-scale phenotypic state variables that integrate organismal traits, community composition and environmental forcing — as the empirical basis for this shift. Continuous functional observables operationalize the leaf economics spectrum and the global plant form–function spectrum by converting their static, species-mean axes into dynamic, intraspecific trajectories. They make intraspecific trait variability and dynamic functional diversity observable at landscape scale for the first time. We outline how imaging spectroscopy provides trait anchors, dense multispectral archives carry the temporal support, and active sensors complement canopy structure; identify falsifiable predictions for trait fingerprints of climate stressors; and illustrate the framework with a European forest example anchored to the 2018 European drought. The novelty is conceptual, not technological: redefining what an ecological state variable is in a warming world.

### Keywords (NEE-shaped)
`functional traits` · `intraspecific trait variability` · `ecosystem resilience` · `functional recovery` · `leaf economics spectrum` · `dynamic functional diversity` · `imaging spectroscopy` · `climate extremes`

> Climate extremes are reshaping ecosystem function, not only ecosystem cover. ### Section 3 — Trait space as the ecological state space of ecosystems (NEW THEORETICAL)

**This is the single most important new section.** It's where the manuscript earns its NEE Perspective status.

**Content (~700 words):**

**§3.1 The static trait ecology canon**
- LES (Wright 2004): leaf economics organized along acquisition–conservation axis
- Global form–function spectrum (Díaz 2016): six dimensions, plant scale + leaf economics
- Reich 2014, Joswig 2022: extensions and refinements
- All built on **species-mean, cross-species, static** trait values

**§3.2 The missing temporal dimension**
- Climate change asks: how does the trait state of *a given community* change *across years*, *across seasons*, *during and after an event*?
- This is not addressed by static species-mean axes
- It requires intraspecific, sub-seasonal, landscape-scale trait observation — historically unattainable at scale

**§3.3 Continuous functional observables as dynamic extension**
- Define sharper (replace current Box definition):
> A continuous functional observable is a **landscape-scale phenotypic state variable**: an emergent property of plant communities that integrates organismal traits, community composition and environmental forcing, and whose temporal trajectory carries information about ecosystem function that is not reducible to any single underlying trait at the organism level. Operationally, it is a spatially and temporally explicit estimate of a plant functional trait with quantified uncertainty and documented domain of applicability.

- They are not the RS implementation of LES — they are LES's **temporal extension**
- They do not replace species-mean trait values — they make trait *trajectories* and *distributions* observable for the first time

**§3.4 What new ecological questions become tractable**
- Does heatwave break the LES trade-off temporarily? (testable)
- Does drought legacy persist in LMA after greenness recovery? (testable)
- Is community functional diversity stable across years, or does it track climate variability? (testable)
- Do different forest types occupy different trajectories in trait space after the same stressor? (testable)

**Citations to add in this section**
Wright 2004, Díaz 2016, Reich 2014, Joswig 2022, Violle 2012, Siefert 2015 (also used in §7)

### Section 6 — Functional recovery, legacy and resilience in trait space (EXPAND)

**Add resilience theory paragraph** (currently missing entirely):
> Resilience ecology has long defined disturbance response as the joint behaviour of resistance (deviation during disturbance), recovery (return trajectory) and legacy (persistent post-disturbance offset) (Pimm 1984; Holling 1973; Hodgson et al. 2015; Donohue et al. 2016). These constructs have been applied across communities, populations and ecosystems, but their empirical implementations from remote sensing have largely substituted greenness anomalies for the underlying functional state. Trait time series provide the missing measurement layer: resistance becomes the magnitude of trait deviation, recovery becomes return in trait space, and legacy becomes the residual trait offset after greenness has returned to baseline.

**Sharper definition of functional recovery**:
> Functional recovery occurs when the post-disturbance trajectory returns to the pre-disturbance trait state or trait distribution within uncertainty, not merely when greenness returns to baseline. This definition makes "greenness recovery without functional recovery" both observable and falsifiable.

**Add Fig 4 LES-plane state-space panel** (see Figure plan below).

**Connect to broader resilience literature**:
- Smith 2022 (already cited, on resilience)
- Donohue 2016 on multi-dimensional resilience
- Hodgson 2015 on "what we don't (yet) know about resilience"

### Section 7 — Dynamic functional diversity and intraspecific variability (NEW MAJOR EXPANSION)

**Current state**: Section 5.3 is ~150 words. **Target**: ~600 words.

**Structure:**

**§7.1 From mean trait maps to trait distributions**
- Trait maps are commonly summarized by per-pixel means
- But the ecological signal lives in distributions: community-weighted means, variance, dispersion, evenness, redundancy
- Continuous functional observables can preserve trait *distributions* in space and time, not just means

**§7.2 Intraspecific trait variability at landscape scale (CRITICAL NEW CONTRIBUTION)**
- Violle et al. 2012 / Albert et al. 2010 / Siefert et al. 2015 / Westerband et al. 2021: ITV is a hidden dimension of community ecology
- ITV has been quantified mostly through field campaigns → plot-bound, slow, sparse
- Trait observables make ITV observable at landscape and seasonal scales for the first time
- This is **not** just methodological — it changes which ITV-related hypotheses are testable:
  - Is ITV greater under climate stress than in stable conditions? (response diversity)
  - Does ITV decline before mean trait shifts (early-warning signal)?
  - Are ITV-rich communities more functionally stable?

**§7.3 Functional diversity dynamics**
- Functional dispersion (Mason 2005, Laliberté 2010)
- Response diversity (Mori 2013, Oliver 2015)
- Functional redundancy (Pillar 2013)
- Dynamic versions of these metrics become observable through continuous trait observables

**§7.4 Earth system model evaluation (keep existing)**
- Trait observables provide constraints for ecosystem models beyond PFT-static parameterizations
- CMIP7 and JULES/CLM/ORCHIDEE benchmarking

**Add NEW Box 5**: "From trait maps to functional diversity observables"
- Community-weighted mean traits
- Functional dispersion / evenness / divergence
- Temporal trait turnover
- ITV at landscape scale
- Response diversity
- Functional redundancy

**Citations to add**: Violle 2012, Siefert 2015, Westerband 2021, Mason 2005, Laliberté 2010, Mori 2013, Oliver 2015, Pillar 2013, Jetz 2016, Schweiger 2018
